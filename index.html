Bills & consultations in progress

Taxi & PHV licensing consolidation — DfT consultation

LicensingClosed — response awaitedFlagged

DfT consulted on cutting England's 263 taxi/PHV licensing authorities down to around 70 local transport authorities, aimed squarely at "out-of-area" working (drivers licensed in one area, operating almost entirely in another — Wolverhampton was cited at ~96%). Follows Baroness Casey's national audit on group-based child sexual exploitation, which flagged cross-border licensing as a safeguarding gap. The 12-week consultation closed 1 April 2026. No formal response yet, but in its 20 October 2026 response to the Transport Committee (below) government said it will respond on transferring licensing to local transport authorities by the end of 2026, and signalled that drivers "should ordinarily be licensed" where they mainly work, without yet committing to a statutory restriction. Directly upstream of any Swindon taxi/PHV policy review.

National minimum standards for taxi/PHV licensing — government response & draft Bill

LicensingGovt response 20 Oct 2026 — draft Bill promisedFlagged

Moved on this refresh. The government published its response to the Commons Transport Committee's "Raising the standard" report (10 June 2026, 18 recommendations) on 20 October 2026. Headlines: a draft Taxis and Private Hire Vehicles Bill will be published for pre-legislative scrutiny during the current session (no date yet), expected to cover enforcement responsibilities, out-of-area working and information-sharing between authorities, while keeping the taxi/PHV distinction. Core safety and safeguarding standards will be set nationally (using powers in the English Devolution and Community Empowerment Act 2026), with some local flexibility elsewhere — government only "partially agreed" with the Committee's warning that standards must not be "low by default". A national standards consultation is due before the end of 2026 and will cover mandatory in-vehicle CCTV (not yet decided), driver competence including English-language proficiency, and disability-awareness training. A national licensing database (valid licences, issuing authority, past refusals/suspensions/revocations) is in a discovery phase running autumn 2026 to spring 2027. On out-of-area working, government says drivers "should ordinarily be licensed" where they do most of their work but has not committed to a statutory restriction. This sets the direction for Swindon's taxi/PHV policy over the next 12–18 months; worth responding to the standards consultation when it opens.

Premises closure orders — duration extended to 12 months

Trading StandardsPower enacted — not yet commencedFlagged

Home Office consultation proposed raising the maximum duration of a premises closure order under the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 from six months (three months plus a three-month extension) to twelve months, cutting the administrative burden of repeat court applications for persistently law-breaking premises. Consultation ran 22 June–31 July 2026; the Prime Minister then publicly committed to taking the change forward (12 August 2026), which CTSI — which had campaigned for it, citing near-universal support among its members — welcomed as "a victory for CTSI's recent campaigning." Update this refresh: the Crime and Policing Act 2026 (Royal Assent 29 April 2026) contains, at s.3(5), a power for the Secretary of State to amend the maximum duration of closure orders. Its Commencement No. 3 Regulations bring a wider set of ASB changes into force today, 26 October 2026: longer closure notices (ordinary 24→48 hours, enhanced 48→72 hours), longer dispersal directions (48→72 hours), and closure powers for registered social housing providers. But s.3(5) is not commenced by those regulations, so the 12-month closure order still awaits further commencement and regulations. CTSI notes the change would still not extend to county councils in two-tier areas or to Scotland, where closure-order powers remain more limited — worth watching if Swindon (a unitary authority) wants to make early use of the extended power once it lands.

Court rulings & appeals

R (application re TfL PHV licence renewal delays) — High Court permission granted

LicensingHigh CourtFlagged

Mr Justice Sweeting granted permission for judicial review of Transport for London's private hire licence renewal process, on evidence that around 17,000 drivers had experienced licence expiry while renewal applications were still pending, with reported financial hardship. The court held it arguable both that TfL must process renewals timeously to avoid interrupting lawful employment, and that TfL's own published guidance may create enforceable expectations about renewal timelines — believed to be the first case testing these points since the Private Hire Vehicles (London) Act 1998. TfL's statutory regime differs from most licensing authorities outside London (Swindon included), so this does not itself bind other authorities, but it is a live marker on how far a licensing authority's own turnaround-time guidance can become legally enforceable — worth watching through to full hearing.

Private hire driver loses High Court appeal over email service of revocation notice

LicensingHigh Court

Sir Peter Lane upheld a licensing authority's email service of a licence-revocation notice as valid, rejecting the driver's claim that it had gone unseen in a spam folder — the email had also reached his solicitor, and he had later replied within the same chain. Confirms that email is a valid, sufficient method of service without prior agreement where the evidence supports actual or constructive receipt, that the 21-day statutory appeal window runs from the date of that service, and that courts will scrutinise "I never saw it" claims closely against the surrounding evidence. A useful reminder for Swindon licensing officers to keep clear records of what was sent, to whom, and any subsequent contact.

D.E.L.T.A. Merseyside Ltd v Uber Britannia Ltd [2025] UKSC 31

LicensingSupreme CourtFlagged

Supreme Court unanimously rejected Uber's argument that private hire operators outside London and Plymouth must contract as principal directly with passengers for every booking. The Local Government (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1976 imposes no such requirement, the Court held — leaving current PHV operator contracting models across England (Swindon included) undisturbed, at least until the licensing-reform proposals above are settled.

LicensingCrown CourtFlagged

Hope and Glory Public House Ltd v Westminster Magistrates' Court [2011] EWCA Civ 31

LicensingLeading authority

Not new, but still the governing Court of Appeal authority on licensing appeals: the appellant carries the burden of persuading the court that the licensing authority's decision was wrong, and the authority's original judgment is owed real weight rather than being reheard from scratch. Every "fit and proper person" appeal decision above — and any Swindon licensing appeal — is decided against this framework.

Coming into force / secondary legislation awaited

English Devolution and Community Empowerment Act 2026 — cross-border taxi/PHV suspension powers

LicensingEnacted — commencement awaitedFlagged

Received Royal Assent 29 April 2026. Gives the Secretary of State powers to set national taxi and PHV standards (the basis for the standards consultation above), and creates a national enforcement framework letting licensing officers suspend, with immediate effect, a driver, vehicle or private hire operator licence issued by another authority where there is a serious public-safety concern, e.g. deliberate use of unlicensed drivers or vehicles. Government stresses the threshold is high and that working outside the issuing area is not in itself a safety risk; appeals and potential compensation apply where procedures are not followed. Newly surfaced on this refresh. A commencement date for the suspension powers was not found, so treat as pending until commencement regulations are made. Once live, Swindon officers could act against out-of-area vehicles working in the borough.

Tobacco and Vapes Act 2026 — age verification regulations laid

Trading StandardsRegs laid — in force 1 Jan 2027Flagged

Received Royal Assent 29 April 2026; introduces the progressive generational sales ban (no one born on or after 1 Jan 2009 may ever legally be sold tobacco), tighter vape flavour/packaging/display rules, and new powers for TS officers against non-compliant retailers, with mandatory local authority enforcement duties. The English/Welsh commencement picture has moved on this refresh: the Tobacco and Vapes Act 2026 (Age Verification Defence to Underage Sales) (England and Wales) Regulations were laid in September 2026, confirming the age-restriction sales offences and the retailer due-diligence defence take effect 1 January 2027. The defence requires either a physical ID document with recognised security features (photo, date of birth, hologram or UV feature) or a certified Digital Verification Service check at "medium confidence" or above — visual inspection of an unverified digital image is not enough. Scotland's own commencement and transitional regulations were already made (SSI 2026/210 and SSI 2026/213). CTSI has separately secured £10m of government funding for enforcement capacity (apprentice officers, illicit trade work).

Trading StandardsDelayed

Core Part 4 consumer protections (unfair commercial practices reform, drip pricing and fake review bans) came into force 6 April 2025 and remain in force — no change there. Private hire operators — statutory right to work checks on drivers

LicensingIn forceFlagged

Under the Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Act 2025, from 1 October 2026 private hire operators are under a direct statutory duty to carry out right-to-work checks on the drivers they engage, sitting alongside — not replacing — existing local authority licensing checks. The Home Office says the change is meant to fix "ambiguity as to where accountability rests if illegal working is identified", particularly for app/platform-based operator models. Now live: worth checking with Swindon's licensed PHV operators that operator-level checks are running alongside (not instead of) the council's own driver-licensing due diligence.

Gambling Commission deposit-limit rules — Remote Technical Standards, phase 2 now live

LicensingIn forceFlagged

Updated Remote Gambling and Software Technical Standards require licensed remote operators to offer customers "gross deposit limits" — using only that label, given equal or greater prominence than other financial limits, on fixed time frames. Phase 1 took effect October 2025; phase 2 was due 30 June 2026 but the Commission pushed it back to 30 September 2026 after stakeholder feedback on technical development and compliance work. Directly relevant to operator compliance under GB gambling licences. The 30 September 2026 date has now passed and no further delay was reported; treated here as in force, though no Commission notice confirming commencement was found this refresh.

Digital ID for alcohol age verification — now in force

LicensingIn forceFlagged

The Licensing Act 2003 (Mandatory Licensing Conditions) (Amendment) Order 2026 came into force on 15 September 2026 — later than the original "Christmas 2025" commitment but ahead of the Autumn 2026 target given when the SI was laid. Staff at licensed premises may now accept certified digital ID for age verification, provided the premises has an agreement with a registered Digital Verification Service Provider meeting at least "medium confidence" under the DVS trust framework (tied to the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025). Adoption is entirely optional — physical ID remains a fully valid alternative — and existing safeguards against underage sales (fines, licence review, the due-diligence defence) are unchanged. The Office for Digital Identities and Attributes has since published further explanatory guidance (September 2026) clarifying that anyone may present digital ID regardless of appearance, that supervised self-service checkout use is permitted, and that verification must use privacy-preserving, data-minimised checks binding the credential to its presenter. Worth flagging to Swindon licensed premises and door staff as guidance beds in.

Rogue-trader crackdown: Approved Code & Trusted Payments Scheme

Trading StandardsVoluntary schemeFlagged

Government announced (28 August 2026) two voluntary consumer-protection schemes for home improvement work, developed with CTSI as a delivery partner: an Approved Code that traders can sign up to demonstrating higher standards on transparency, customer service and dispute resolution, and a Trusted Payments Scheme that holds building-project funds securely and releases them against project milestones. Prompted by CTSI data showing over one in four UK adults have had problems with home improvement work and £10.3bn lost to scams, overpricing and unfair practices in 2024. The Trusted Payments app launched the following week with a target of 100,000+ traders signed up by end of September 2026; the Approved Code is expected to be fully operational by December 2026. CTSI itself has continued to press for compulsory registration of home-repair tradespeople rather than a voluntary code alone — worth pointing Swindon consumers and traders to both schemes as they roll out, while noting neither is a licensing requirement.

National Licensing Policy Framework — non-statutory steer for licensing authorities

LicensingGuidanceFlagged

Following the 2025 licensing "taskforce" review below, the Home Office and Department for Business and Trade published the National Licensing Policy Framework (26 November 2025) — non-statutory guidance for on-trade Licensing Act 2003 premises (pubs, restaurants, nightclubs, late-night refreshment) that licensing authorities "must have regard to" alongside their existing duties, without displacing them or creating new legal obligations. It asks authorities to weigh "the need to promote growth and deliver economic benefits" when making licensing decisions, and to explain at their next Statement of Licensing Policy review how they have taken account of it, as part of a stated aim to cut regulatory costs by 25%. Government has flagged it may pursue statutory change later (e.g. a growth-related licensing objective) depending on impact — a review of the framework was due six months after publication, i.e. around May 2026; no published outcome of that review has been found as of this refresh. The framework has continued to draw academic criticism over industry influence and reduced local accountability (Nicholls et al., Addiction, 2026). Relevant the next time Swindon's own Statement of Licensing Policy comes up for review.
